Pagination & Index

Step 16 of 21 — Collate, number, bind and index the complete file

Labour Court
What Must Be Paginated?
Rule 37(25) & Rule 29(3) — The complete file in three sections

📖 The Law

Rule 37(25): After receipt of any replying affidavit or the expiry of the time limit for filing a replying affidavit (whichever occurs first), the applicant must index and paginate the file in terms of Rule 29(3) and, within 10 court days, apply to the Registrar for a hearing date.

Rule 29(3): Papers must be arranged in chronological order, consecutively paginated, securely bound, and accompanied by a correct and complete index of evidence and documents.

Rule 29(4): Volumes must not exceed 120 pages (or 200 pages in a lever arch file).

Rule 8: All documents must be A4, 12pt font (Arial or Times New Roman), 1.5 line spacing, 2cm margins.

📋 The Complete File Has Three Sections

Under Rule 37(25), the complete file must be divided into three separate sections, each with its own index:

📁 Section A: Pleadings and Affidavits

All court documents filed by the parties, in chronological order:

  • Notice of Motion (with all annexures)
  • Founding Affidavit (with all annexures)
  • Notice of Intention to Oppose (if any)
  • Answering Affidavit (if any)
  • Replying Affidavit (if any)
  • Supplementary Affidavit (if any)
  • Notice of Standing By (if any)
  • Notice of Amendment (if any)

📄 Section B: Notices and Other Documents

All procedural notices and correspondence, in chronological order:

  • Notice of Set Down (if any)
  • Notices of Filing and Service
  • Proofs of Service
  • Any correspondence with the Registrar
  • Any consent orders or directions

📀 Section C: The Record

The CCMA arbitration record, in chronological order:

  • Arbitration Award
  • Referral Form (LRA 7.11)
  • Employment Contract / Proof of Employment
  • Disciplinary Record / Dismissal Letter
  • Witness Statements / Evidence Led
  • Payslips / Salary Records
  • Arbitration Hearing Transcript / Minutes
  • Commissioner's Notes / Rulings (if available)
⚠️ Important

Each section must have its own index and own consecutive pagination. Do not paginate the entire file as one continuous document. Section A pages 1-50, Section B pages 1-20, Section C pages 1-100 (for example).

If any section exceeds 120 pages, split it into volumes (e.g., Section C Volume 1 of 2, Section C Volume 2 of 2).

Pagination Checklist
Rule 29 & Rule 8 — Ensure your file is court-ready

📖 Rule 29 — Preparation of Papers

  • Pagination: All pages numbered consecutively within each section.
  • Chronological Order: Documents arranged from oldest to newest within each section.
  • Binding: Securely bound in suitable covers. Max 120 pages per volume (200 in lever arch).
  • Index: Correct and complete index of evidence and documents for each section.
  • Format: A4, 12pt font (Arial or Times New Roman), 1.5 line spacing, 2cm margins (Rule 8).
  • All documents in Section A (Pleadings) are in chronological order
  • All documents in Section B (Notices) are in chronological order
  • All documents in Section C (Record) are in chronological order
  • Each section has its own consecutive page numbers (1 to end)
  • No volume exceeds 120 pages (or 200 in lever arch file)
  • Each volume has a cover page: case name, case number, parties, volume number
  • Each section has a complete index with page references
  • Font is Arial or Times New Roman, 12pt, 1.5 spacing, 2cm margins
  • All pages are A4 size
  • Documents are securely bound (stapled, comb-bound, or in lever arch file)
  • I have a separate copy for the Registrar and each Respondent
  • I am ready to apply for a hearing date within 10 court days

Apply for Hearing Date
Rule 37(25) — 10 court days after pleadings close
⏰ 10-DAY DEADLINE — Rule 37(25)

You must apply to the Registrar for a hearing date within 10 court days after indexing and paginating the file. If you miss this deadline, your matter may be delayed or struck from the roll.

📖 The Law

Rule 37(25): After indexing and paginating, the applicant must apply to the Registrar for a hearing date within 10 court days.

Rule 37(26): For unopposed matters, the applicant may apply for a hearing date within 10 court days after the last day for filing an answering affidavit.

Rule 40: Heads of Argument must be filed within 15 court days after pleadings close (for opposed reviews). For unopposed reviews, Heads are still required because it is a review application.

  • Log in to the Court Online Portal at https://courtonline.co.za.
  • Navigate to My Cases → Select your case.
  • Look for "Apply for Hearing Date" or "Set Down" option.
  • Select the type of hearing: Unopposed Motion / Opposed Motion / Review.
  • Upload the complete paginated file (all sections, properly indexed).
  • Confirm that all parties have been served and the proof of service is filed.
  • Enter your digital signature and submit.
  • The Registrar will allocate a hearing date and notify all parties.

📝 What Happens Next?

After applying for a hearing date:

  1. The Registrar allocates a hearing date and notifies all parties;
  2. You must file Heads of Argument within 15 court days (opposed) or before the hearing (unopposed);
  3. Prepare your oral argument for the hearing;
  4. Attend the hearing on the allocated date.
