Helpful Tip: You may record conversations you are part of for your personal records. This can help you remember what was said. However, always inform the other party if you plan to record — transparency is best.
Don't Settle Under Pressure: At conciliation, the commissioner may encourage settlement. Only agree if the terms are fair and you fully understand them. You have the right to take the matter to arbitration if you are not satisfied. Take your time — you can ask for a break to think.

Common Questions

What is the CCMA and what does it do?
The Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) is a free dispute resolution body in South Africa. It handles unfair dismissal, unfair labour practice, and discrimination disputes. The CCMA tries to resolve disputes through conciliation (settlement talks) and, if that fails, arbitration (a formal hearing where a commissioner makes a binding decision).
Is the CCMA online or in-person?
CCMA is IN-PERSON. You must physically attend hearings at a CCMA office or designated venue. Some CCMA offices offer hybrid hearings (partially virtual), but you should expect to attend in person. CCMA does NOT use Court Online — that system is only for the Labour Court. Bring your ID and all relevant documents to every hearing.
What happens if I miss the 30-day deadline?
If you miss the 30-day deadline for unfair dismissal (or 90 days for unfair labour practice), you can apply for condonation — asking the CCMA to accept your late referral. You must explain why you were late and show that you have a good case. Condonation is not guaranteed, so file on time if at all possible. Advome can help you draft a condonation application if needed.
Can I challenge a CCMA award in the Labour Court?
Yes. You have 6 weeks from the date of the award to apply for a review in the Labour Court. This is done via a Section 145 review. Important: Labour Court matters are COMPULSORY online through Court Online. You must register on Court Online and file all documents electronically. Advome guides you through this process — see our Court Online guide.
Do I need an attorney?
No — you can represent yourself at the CCMA. Many people do so successfully. However, complex matters (especially Labour Court reviews) may benefit from legal representation.
