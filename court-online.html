Step 1: Register on Court Online

Before you can file anything, you must create a Court Online account. This is mandatory for all litigants from 14 April 2025.

Mandatory from 14 April 2025 — No new cases may be issued manually (in person). All applications must be initiated on the Court Online Portal. Directive 1.1–1.2
1

Go to the Court Online Portal

Visit courtonline.judiciary.org.za. The Judiciary home page provides FAQs, contact info, registration details, and video tutorials.

Tip: If you do not have a computer or internet access, visit the Court Online Service Desk at the Labour Court. Staff will assist you with registration and filing free of charge. Directive 1.7
2

Click "Register"

Select the registration option for individuals (self-represented litigants). You will need your South African ID number.

Foreign nationals without a South African ID: Contact the Court Online Support Helpdesk at CourtOnlineSupport@judiciary.org.za for assistance. Include your LPC number if you are a registered legal practitioner. Directive 1.4
3

Enter Your Personal Details

Complete the registration form with your full name, South African ID number, physical address, telephone number, and email address. All fields are required.

  • ✓ Full name (as per ID)
  • ✓ South African ID number (13 digits)
  • ✓ Physical residential address
  • ✓ Cell phone number
  • ✓ Valid email address (you will receive activation and case notifications here)
4

Create a Secure Password

Your password must meet the portal's security requirements. Write it down and store it safely.

  • ✓ At least 8 characters
  • ✓ One uppercase letter
  • ✓ One lowercase letter
  • ✓ One number
  • ✓ One special character (!@#$%^&*)
5

Submit Registration

Click the "Register" button. The system will validate your ID number against Home Affairs data. If validation fails, check your ID number and try again, or contact the Help Desk.

6

Activate Your Account

Check your email inbox (and spam/junk folder) for an activation email from Court Online. Click the activation link to verify your account. You cannot log in until this step is complete.

Important: If you do not receive the activation email within 30 minutes, contact the Court Online Support Helpdesk. Do not create a duplicate account. Directive 1.6
7

Complete Your Profile

Log in and complete any remaining profile fields. Ensure your contact details are accurate — the Registrar and other parties will use this information to communicate with you.

Self-Represented Litigants: You are now ready to initiate your case. If you need help at any point, you MUST be referred to the Court Online Service Desk at the court, where designated staff can assist with case initiation free of charge. Directive 1.7

Registration Checklist

Step 2: Issue Your Motion & Obtain a Case Number

This is the most critical step. Upload ONLY your signed Notice of Motion first. The Registrar will issue an official case number. Do NOT upload your Founding Affidavit at this stage.

CRITICAL: Upload ONLY the Notice of Motion for issuing. The Founding Affidavit and all other documents are uploaded AFTER the case number is issued. Uploading the affidavit at this stage will cause rejection. Directive 1.11
Reference Number vs Case Number: When you click "Start a Case," the portal generates a reference number (e.g., HCGS00363). This is NOT the case number. The case number (e.g., J1234/2026) is issued by the Registrar after reviewing your Notice of Motion. Only the case number goes on court documents. Directive 1.8–1.10
1

Log In to Court Online

Go to courtonline.judiciary.org.za and log in with your registered email and password.

2

Click "Start a Case"

On your dashboard, click the "Start a Case" button. The portal will generate a reference number for this draft case. Remember: this is NOT your case number.

Do NOT write the reference number on any court papers. Only the official case number issued by the Registrar may be used on documents. Directive 1.10
3

Select the Correct Court and Case Type

Choose the Labour Court seat where your matter will be heard (Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, or Gqeberha). Select the appropriate case type:

  • ✓ Review Application — if you are reviewing a CCMA or bargaining council award (Rule 37)
  • ✓ Referral — if you are referring a dispute in terms of section 145 of the LRA
  • ✓ Application — for other applications (e.g., interdict, contempt)
4

Enter Party Details

Enter the full names of all parties. For a review application:

  • ✓ Applicant: Your full name (the employee)
  • ✓ First Respondent: The CCMA or bargaining council
  • ✓ Second Respondent: Your employer
Tip: Ensure all names are spelled exactly as they appear in the arbitration award and on official documents. Mismatches cause delays.
5

Upload ONLY the Notice of Motion

This is the single most important step. Upload only your signed Notice of Motion as a PDF. The Notice of Motion must:

  • ✓ Be in PDF format
  • ✓ Be duly signed by you (the applicant)
  • ✓ Call upon the respondent to show cause why the award should not be reviewed
  • ✓ Call upon the CCMA/bargaining council to dispatch the complete record within 10 days
  • ✓ NOT include the Founding Affidavit at this stage
Do NOT upload the Founding Affidavit now. The affidavit is filed later under "My Case Documents" after the case number is issued. Directive 1.11
6

Submit for Issuing

Click "Submit" or "Issue." Your request is now sent to the Registrar for review. The portal will display your reference number and a confirmation message.

What happens next: The Registrar reviews your Notice of Motion for compliance with Rule 7 and the Directive. If compliant, the Registrar approves and issues an official case number. If non-compliant, you will receive an email explaining the defect. Directive 1.9
7

Wait for the Case Number

The Registrar usually issues the case number within 1–3 business days. You will receive an email notification when the case is issued.

Check your email and portal dashboard regularly. Once issued, the case number (e.g., J1234/2026) will appear on your dashboard under "My Cases." This is the number you must use on all subsequent documents.
8

Download the Issued Notice of Motion

Once the case number is issued, download the stamped/endorsed Notice of Motion from the portal. This is the version you must serve on the respondents. It now bears the official case number.

9

Serve the Notice of Motion

Serve the issued Notice of Motion on all respondents within the timeframes prescribed by the Labour Court Rules:

  • ✓ Rule 9(1)(a)(iv): Service by email is sufficient if the respondent has an email address
  • ✓ Rule 9(1)(a)(i): Personal service at the respondent's place of business or residence
  • ✓ Rule 9(1)(a)(ii): Delivery to a person apparently over 16 at the respondent's place of business or residence
  • ✓ Rule 9(1)(a)(iii): Service by registered post
File an Affidavit of Service: After serving, you must file an affidavit confirming how, when, and on whom service was effected. Upload this to Court Online under "My Case Documents." Rule 9

Issuing Checklist

Step 3: File Your Documents

After the case number is issued, upload all remaining documents under "My Case Documents." All documents must be in PDF format. Select the correct document type from the dropdown.

All documents must be in PDF format. Do not upload Word, Excel, or image files. The correct document type must be selected when filing. Do NOT select "Other" — email the Help Desk if your document type is missing. Directive 1.12(a)–(e)
1

Navigate to "My Case Documents"

Log in to Court Online, go to your case dashboard, and click on your case. Select "My Case Documents" to view the document upload area.

2

Upload the Founding Affidavit

Select "Founding Affidavit" from the document type dropdown. Upload your signed Founding Affidavit in PDF format. This affidavit sets out the grounds for your review application.

Rule 37(2)(c): The Notice of Motion must be supported by an affidavit setting out the grounds on which the applicant relies to have the decision reviewed and corrected or set aside.
3

Upload the Affidavit of Service

Select "Affidavit of Service" from the document type dropdown. Upload the affidavit confirming that the Notice of Motion was properly served on all respondents.

  • ✓ State the date and time of service
  • ✓ State the place of service
  • ✓ State the name of the person served (or that service was by email/registered post)
  • ✓ Attach proof of service (email read receipt, registered post tracking, etc.)
4

Upload the Arbitration Award

Select "Arbitration Award" from the document type dropdown. Upload the certified copy of the CCMA or bargaining council award you are reviewing.

5

CCMA Dispatches the Record (Rule 37)

Upon receipt of your Notice of Motion, the CCMA or bargaining council must deliver the complete record of arbitration proceedings to the Registrar through a cloud-based service. Directive 2.1; Rule 37(2)(b)

What you must do: The CCMA/bargaining council uploads the Rule 37(2) notice and bundle to Court Online. The Registrar retrieves the digital record and stores it on the court's SharePoint. You will receive a SharePoint link to access the record. Directive 2.2–2.6
6

Upload the Record (If Applicable)

Once you receive the SharePoint link from the Registrar, you may need to upload portions of the record to your case file. Follow the Registrar's instructions.

Rule 37(13): The applicant must furnish the Registrar and each other party with a copy of the record. If the respondent failed to deliver a complete record, the 60-day period for filing answering affidavits only starts once the complete record is delivered.
7

Upload the Answering Affidavit (If Defended)

If the respondent files an answering affidavit, they must upload it to Court Online. You will be notified by email. You then have the opportunity to file a replying affidavit.

Timeframes: The respondent has 10 days after service to file an answering affidavit (Rule 37(14)). You then have 10 days to file a replying affidavit. Monitor your email and portal dashboard closely.
8

Document Formatting Requirements (Rule 8)

All documents uploaded to Court Online must comply with Rule 8 of the Labour Court Rules:

  • ✓ A4 size paper
  • ✓ One-sided printing
  • ✓ Font size 12 or larger
  • ✓ Double-spaced text
  • ✓ Margins: at least 25mm on all sides
  • ✓ Pages numbered consecutively
  • ✓ Bound or stapled on the left margin
  • ✓ PDF format only

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I file manually at the court instead of using Court Online?

No. As of 14 April 2025, Directive 1 of 2025 makes Court Online mandatory for all new Labour Court cases. Manual issuing is prohibited. Directive 1.1–1.2

What if I don't have a computer or internet access?

Visit the Court Online Service Desk at the Labour Court. Staff will assist you with registration and filing free of charge. Directive 1.7

What is the difference between the reference number and case number?

The reference number (e.g., HCGS00363) is generated by Court Online when you click "Start a Case." The case number (e.g., J1234/2026) is issued by the Registrar after approving your Notice of Motion. Only the case number goes on court documents. Directive 1.8–1.10

Can I upload the Founding Affidavit with the Notice of Motion when starting the case?

No. Upload ONLY the Notice of Motion for issuing. The Founding Affidavit is uploaded later under "My Case Documents" after the case number is issued. Uploading the affidavit at the start will cause rejection. Directive 1.11

How long does the Registrar take to issue a case number?

Usually 1–3 business days, depending on volume and compliance. You will receive an email notification.

Is there a fee for using Court Online?

No. Registration, filing, and document uploads are free for all users. The Labour Court does not charge filing fees for review applications by self-represented litigants.

What if the document type I need is not in the dropdown?

Do NOT select "Other." Email CourtOnlineSupport@judiciary.org.za with the subject "Request to add a new document type on Court Online" and describe the document. Directive 1.12(d)–(e)

Can I serve documents by email?

Yes. Under Labour Court Rule 9(1)(a)(iv), service by email is sufficient if the other party has an email address. You must file an affidavit of service proving delivery. Rule 9(1)(a)(iv)
