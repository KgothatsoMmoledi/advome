Understanding Your Deadline

The most common reason CCMA cases fail — missing the deadline

30-Day Rule for Unfair Dismissal

You have 30 calendar days from the date of dismissal to refer your dispute to the CCMA. This includes weekends and public holidays. The clock starts the day after your dismissal date. If the 30th day falls on a Sunday or public holiday, the deadline extends to the next working day.

What counts as the "date of dismissal"?

With notice: The date your notice period expired, or the date you were paid all outstanding salary — whichever is earlier.
Without notice: The date your contract terminated or the date you left the employer's service — whichever is earlier.
Constructive dismissal: The date you resigned due to the employer's conduct.
After appeal: The date the employer made the final decision to uphold the dismissal.

90-Day Rule for Unfair Labour Practice

If your dispute is about an unfair labour practice (e.g., unfair suspension, demotion, discrimination, benefits dispute) — not a dismissal — you have 90 days from the date of the act or omission to refer the dispute.

LRA Form 7.11 — Field-by-Field Guide

Plain-language explanations for every section of the referral form

LRA Form 7.11 is the official form used to refer a dispute to the CCMA for conciliation (and potentially con-arb). It is a 5-page form. Below is a simplified guide to completing each section correctly. You can download the official form from the CCMA website or obtain it at any CCMA office.

1 Details of Party Referring the Dispute Required

Fill in your full name, ID number, postal address, and contact details. If you are a trade union or employer's organisation referring on behalf of a member, provide the organisation's details here. Tip: Use a permanent address where you can reliably receive mail — the CCMA will send notices to this address.

Provide an alternative contact (friend, family member, or union rep) in case you change your phone number.
2 Details of the Other Party Required

Provide the full legal name of your employer, their registered address, and contact details. If you are unsure of the exact registered name, check your payslip, employment contract, or letter of appointment. If there are multiple parties (e.g., a labour broker and client), list all of them.

Double-check the employer's registered name on CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) if you are unsure — using the wrong name can cause delays.
3 Nature of the Dispute Critical

Tick only ONE box. This determines the entire path of your case. Options include:

  • Unfair dismissal — You were fired, retrenched, or forced to resign (constructive dismissal)
  • Unfair labour practice — Suspension, demotion, unfair benefits, etc. (not a dismissal)
  • Unfair discrimination — Discrimination based on race, gender, pregnancy, disability, etc.
  • Organisational rights — Trade union access, stop-order facilities, etc.
  • Refusal to bargain — Employer refuses to negotiate with a recognised union
  • Severance pay / S41 BCEA — Dispute about severance pay under the Basic Conditions of Employment Act
Choosing the wrong category can get your case dismissed. If you are unsure, use the AI chat assistant below or consult an attorney.
4 Summary of the Facts Required

Write a brief, factual summary of what happened. Stick to the key facts: dates, what occurred, and why you believe it was unfair. Avoid emotional language or legal arguments — you will have a chance to present your full case later. Keep it under 200 words if possible.

Example: "I was dismissed on 15 March 2026. I was accused of theft. No disciplinary hearing was held. I was told to leave immediately. I deny the allegation and was never given a chance to respond."
5 Date the Dispute Arose Critical

This is the date that starts the clock for your deadline. For unfair dismissal, this is the date of dismissal. For unfair labour practice, it is the date the unfair act occurred. This date must match your supporting documents.

6 Details of Dispute Procedures Followed Required

State whether you followed any internal grievance or disciplinary appeal procedures before coming to the CCMA. If yes, describe the steps you took and the outcome. If no, explain why (e.g., no internal procedure existed, or the employer refused to entertain an appeal).

7 Outcome Required Required

State what remedy you are seeking. Common options:

  • Reinstatement — Get your job back with full back pay (most common for unfair dismissal)
  • Re-employment — Get your job back but on new terms
  • Compensation — Money award (capped at 12 months remuneration for ordinary unfair dismissal; up to 24 months for automatically unfair dismissal)
  • Other — E.g., withdrawal of warning, restoration of benefits, etc.
8 Sector / Industry Optional

Indicate the sector in which you worked (e.g., retail, mining, domestic, public service). This helps the CCMA assign a commissioner with relevant experience and ensures the correct bargaining council is identified if applicable.

9 Interpretation Services Optional

If you require an interpreter (for any of South Africa's 11 official languages), tick "Yes" and specify the language. The CCMA provides interpreters free of charge for official languages. If you need an interpreter for a non-official language, you may bring one at your own cost.

10 Objection to Con-Arb Optional

Con-arb means conciliation followed immediately by arbitration on the same day if conciliation fails. If you want to object to con-arb (giving you more time to prepare for arbitration), tick the box and sign. You cannot object in certain disputes (e.g., dismissal for misconduct, incapacity, or operational requirements under Section 191(5A)).

Should you object? If your case is complex, you need more witnesses, or you want time to gather more evidence after conciliation, consider objecting. If your case is straightforward and you want a faster resolution, allow con-arb.
11 Part B — Dismissal Details (if applicable) If Dismissal

If your dispute is about unfair dismissal, you must complete Part B (page 5 of the form). This includes:

  • Date of employment commencement — When you started working
  • Notice of dismissal — How you were informed (in writing, orally, or other)
  • Reason for dismissal — Misconduct, incapacity, operational requirements (retrenchment), unknown, or constructive dismissal
  • Procedural fairness — Was a fair procedure followed? (Yes/No + explanation)
  • Substantive fairness — Was the reason for dismissal fair? (Yes/No + explanation)

Signature Requirements

The form must be signed by the referring party (you, the employee). If a trade union or employer's organisation is referring on your behalf, they must sign. If there are multiple employees in the same dispute and no union is involved, each employee must sign a separate page attached to the form. Electronic signatures are accepted for online filings.

Where to File Your Referral

CCMA is IN-PERSON — file at the correct regional office

CCMA is IN-PERSON

The CCMA does not have a fully online filing system for all matters. You must either hand-deliver your form to the CCMA office, fax it, email it to the correct regional office, or send it by registered post. Online filing is available via the CCMA's electronic portal for some matters, but you should confirm receipt. Always keep proof of filing.

Filing Methods

1. Hand delivery: Take the form to the CCMA regional office where the dispute arose. Get a stamped receipt.
2. Email: Scan and email to the relevant regional office email address (see below). Request a read receipt.
3. Fax: Fax to the regional office fax number. Keep the fax transmission slip.
4. Registered post: Send to the regional office postal address. Keep the postal receipt. Presumed received 7 days after posting.
5. Online: Use the CCMA electronic referral portal at cmonline.ccma.org.za (confirm availability for your dispute type).

Proof of Service on the Employer is MANDATORY

You must serve a copy of the completed referral form (and condonation application, if applicable) on the employer before or at the same time as filing with the CCMA. You must attach proof of service to your CCMA filing. Acceptable proof: registered mail receipt, fax confirmation slip, email read receipt, signed hand-delivery receipt, or an affidavit of service.

CCMA Regional Offices

File at the CCMA office in the province where the dispute arose (usually where you worked).
