Reference Number ≠ Case Number (Directive 1 of 2025)

Court Online generates a reference number when you initiate the case. This is NOT the case number. The Registrar will issue the actual case number after reviewing your Notice of Motion. Only use the case number on all subsequent documents.

Service for Review — No Sheriff Required

Under Labour Court Rules, service for review applications does not require a sheriff. Email service to a working address that respondents actually use is sufficient. Ensure you can prove delivery (read receipt, delivery confirmation, or screenshot).

Court Online Registration & Filing

Compulsory e-filing for all Labour Court matters (Directive 1 of 2025)

Directive 1 of 2025 — All New Cases Must Be Filed Online

From 14 April 2025, no new Labour Court cases may be issued manually. All matters, including those by unrepresented litigants, must be initiated on the Court Online Portal. Unrepresented litigants can receive assistance at the Court Online Service Desk at the Labour Court.

Step-by-Step Registration

1
Register at Court Online
Visit courtonline.judiciary.org.za. Select "Individual" and use your South African ID number. Foreign nationals without SA ID must contact the Help Desk for assistance.
2
Verify Your Email
Check your inbox for a verification link. Click it to activate your account. This is required before you can file any documents.
3
Sign In & Start a Case
Log in, click "Start a Case". Select Labour Court and choose the correct seat (Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, or Gqeberha). Ensure you select the seat where the arbitration took place or where the employer operates.
4
Upload ONLY the Signed Notice of Motion
Upload only your signed Notice of Motion (PDF) to obtain a reference number. Do NOT upload the Founding Affidavit yet. The Registrar needs to review and issue a case number first. Reference number ≠ Case number.
5
Wait for Case Number
The Registrar will review your Notice of Motion and issue a formal case number via email. This may take 1–3 business days. You will receive an email notification when the case is issued.
6
Upload Remaining Documents
Once the case number is issued, log in to "My Case Documents" and upload: (1) Founding Affidavit, (2) Arbitration Award, (3) Any other supporting documents. Select the correct document type for each upload. Do NOT select "Other" — contact the Help Desk if the type is missing.
7
Pay Filing Fees
Pay the required filing fees. See the fee table below. If you cannot afford fees, you may apply for indigent status under Rule 70.

Indigent Status (Rule 70)

If you cannot afford the filing fees, you may apply for indigent status. Complete Form 2 of the Rules and file it with your Notice of Motion. The Registrar will assess your financial circumstances. If granted, all fees are waived.

Unrepresented Litigant Assistance (Directive 1 of 2025, para 1.7)

Unrepresented litigants must be referred to the Court Online Service Desk at the Labour Court, where designated staff can assist with case initiation. A record of all assisted cases is kept by the Registrar.

Note on Unopposed Matters

Even though the matter is unopposed, you must still file the complete record and apply for set-down. The Court will hear the matter in your favour unless there are obvious defects in your papers.

Responding Affidavit Received

The respondent(s) have filed an answering affidavit under Rule 7(4). You now have the opportunity to file a replying affidavit under Rule 7(5).

Replying Affidavit Rules (Rule 7(5)(a))

Your replying affidavit must:

  • Address ONLY new issues raised in the responding affidavit
  • Not introduce new facts that were not in your founding affidavit
  • Be filed within 5 days of receiving the responding affidavit
  • Be concise and focused on rebuttal

Preparation Resources

LRA s145(1): Any party to a dispute may apply to the Labour Court to review any award or ruling of the CCMA or bargaining council.

Rule 37(2): The notice of motion must call upon the person/body to show cause; call upon the CCMA to dispatch the complete record to the Registrar within 10 days; and be supported by a concise affidavit.

Rule 37(4): The affidavit must contain no more than a concise statement of grounds in point form.

Rule 7(2)(e): Respondents must deliver an answering affidavit within 10 days after service.

Under Rule 37(4), your founding affidavit must be concise. It should NOT repeat all evidence, make abstract legal statements, or introduce new facts. It MUST state each error in point form, reference the award, explain why the result is unreasonable, and attach the award.

Example: "The commissioner erred because: (a) failed to consider the disciplinary code requires a first written warning; (b) ignored uncontradicted evidence the incident was not intentional."

For Labour Court review, service does not require a sheriff. You may serve by email to a working address, registered post, or personal delivery. Keep proof: email read receipts, screenshots, tracking numbers, or acknowledgment forms.
