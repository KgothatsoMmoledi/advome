Compulsory Online Filing
⚖️

Labour Court Directive 1 of 2025 — Effective 14 April 2025

All Labour Court applications MUST be filed electronically via Court Online. No physical filing is permitted unless authorised by the Judge. Unrepresented litigants may use the Court Online Service Desk at the Labour Court for assistance.

⚠️ Important: Reference Number ≠ Case Number

When you "Start a Case" on Court Online, you get a reference number (temporary). Only after the Registrar approves your Notice of Motion do you receive the official case number. Do NOT write the reference number on any court documents.

💡 Cost-Saving Tip

If you cannot afford fees, you may apply for indigent status (Rule 70). Contact the Registrar with proof of income. The Court may waive filing fees and appoint a pro bono attorney.

Preparation Resources

  1. Register on Court Online at courtonline.co.za. Select "Register as an Individual." Use your SA ID number. Foreign nationals: contact the Help Desk.
  2. Verify your email — click the activation link sent to your inbox.
  3. Sign in and click "Start a Case" from the left menu.
  4. Select the Court — choose "Labour Court" and the correct seat (Johannesburg / Cape Town / Durban / Gqeberha).
  5. Upload ONLY the signed Notice of Motion (PDF). This is to get your case number. Do NOT upload the affidavit yet.
  6. Wait for the Registrar to issue the case number. You will receive an email notification.
  7. Once case number is issued, go to "My Cases," open your case, and upload your Founding Affidavit and other documents under "My Case Documents."
  8. Pay filing fees via the Court Online payment portal (EFT or card).
🚨 Critical Rule

Directive 1 of 2025, para 1.11: You must upload only the Notice of Motion to get a case number. Uploading the affidavit at this stage will cause the Registrar to reject your application. The affidavit comes AFTER the case is issued.

Rule 7(2) and Rule 37(2) require the following in your Notice of Motion:

Day 0: You upload Notice of Motion → Registrar issues case number.

Day 0–10: CCMA/Commissioner must dispatch the arbitration record to the Registrar (Rule 37(2)(b)). If they do not, apply for an order compelling compliance (Rule 37(4)).

Day 0–10: You serve the Notice of Motion on all respondents (Employer, CCMA, Commissioner). File proof of service on Court Online.

Day 10–20: Respondent may file Notice of Intention to Oppose + Answering Affidavit (Rule 7(4)(b)).

Day 20–25: You may file Replying Affidavit (Rule 7(5)(a)) — only address new issues raised, no new facts.

After pleadings close: File Heads of Argument (Rule 40(2)) — 15 days after close of pleadings for unopposed; 15 days before hearing for opposed.

Then: Apply for set-down (enrolment) via Court Online.

Legal Basis

LRA Section 145
Gives you the right to review an arbitration award within 6 weeks. The Labour Court may review awards that are unreasonable, procedurally irregular, or exceed the Commissioner's powers.
Labour Court Rule 37
Governs review applications. Requires Notice of Motion calling upon the CCMA and Commissioner to show cause, supported by affidavit setting out factual and legal grounds.
Labour Court Rule 7
General application rules. Notice of Motion must contain title, case number, relief sought, address for service, and 10-day answering notice.
Directive 1 of 2025
All Labour Court matters must be filed via Court Online. Unrepresented litigants may use the Service Desk. Reference numbers are NOT case numbers.
