What is a Motion for Leave?

A Motion for Leave to Appeal is an application asking the Labour Court for permission to appeal the arbitration award. The respondent will have an opportunity to oppose your application. The Court will decide whether your appeal has reasonable prospects of success.

What is Default Judgment?

When a respondent fails to file opposing papers within the time allowed (usually 10 days after service), the applicant may apply for default judgment. This means the Court may grant the relief sought without a hearing, because the respondent has not defended the matter.

Important Timing

You must wait until the respondent's time to file opposing papers has expired. Do not apply for default judgment before the deadline. The Court will check that proper service was effected.

If Defended (Motion for Leave):

  1. You file your Motion for Leave and supporting affidavit on Court Online
  2. The respondent may file a Notice of Intention to Oppose (within 10 days)
  3. The respondent files opposing affidavit(s)
  4. You may file a replying affidavit (if necessary)
  5. The Court sets a hearing date or decides on the papers
  6. The Court grants or refuses leave to appeal

If Undefended (Default Judgment):

  1. You file your Notice of Motion and supporting affidavit on Court Online
  2. You attach proof that the respondent was served and did not respond
  3. The Court reviews your papers
  4. If satisfied, the Court grants default judgment in your favour
  5. If not satisfied, the Court may require a hearing or further proof

The Labour Court will only grant leave to appeal if your appeal has reasonable prospects of success. This is a legal standard that means:

  • There is a reasonable chance that the Labour Appeal Court will overturn the arbitration award
  • You must identify a legal error (not just a factual disagreement)
  • The error must be material — it must have affected the outcome

Examples of reasonable prospects:

  • The arbitrator misinterpreted LRA s188 (fair reason requirements)
  • The arbitrator applied the wrong legal test for constructive dismissal
  • The arbitrator ignored binding precedent (e.g., NUMSA v. Bader Bop)
  • The arbitrator's decision is irrational or logically indefensible

Not reasonable prospects: "I disagree with the outcome" or "The arbitrator didn't believe me." These are factual disputes, not legal errors.

Motion Checklist

  • Review application filed on Court Online
  • Respondent served with review application
  • Waited for respondent's time to expire (10 days)
  • Determined if respondent responded or not
  • Drafted motion documents (using this tool)
  • Have proof of service ready
